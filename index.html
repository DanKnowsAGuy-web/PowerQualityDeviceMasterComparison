Master Feature & Mechanism Comparison

All products in this table address power quality problems but operate through fundamentally different mechanisms and target different electrical phenomena. Your portfolio products are in the first four columns. Competitor/reference products follow. SATIC's three wire-in sizes share the same core mechanism and differ by capacity and panel configuration only.

SATIC Size Note: The Standard Power Perfect Box (60 µF / 18 modules, 120/240V), Super Duty Power Perfect Box (120 µF / 36 modules, 120/240V), and Three-Phase Heavy Duty Box (165 µF, 277/480V) share identical operating principles. Selection is driven by panel voltage and facility load size. The Pure Power Plug-In (ES120) is the same capacitive mechanism in a 120V plug-in form for single-circuit or supplemental deployment.

Stabl Power Series Note: Stabl Power Series 1–3 correspond to Electric Panel Harmonizer Pro Series 1–3. Series 1 (240,000A, up to 100KW) is secondary protection only and must be installed downline from a primary-level unit. Series 2 and 3 (335,000A each, up to 250KW) can stand alone in most commercial environments. Series 4–6 (500K–2M amps, up to 2,500KW) are primary-level IEEE Cat. C main switchgear units available for larger industrial deployments through Energy Plus.

02

Facility-Type Deployment Recommendations

Each profile identifies the dominant power quality problems present, the primary recommendation, the role of each device, and the variables that would change the recommendation. Recommendations now reflect a three-device framework: Tune (harmonic reduction), Stabl Power (primary surge protection and transient elimination), and SATIC (power factor correction and demand charge reduction). Based on harmonic load density, typical billing structure, equipment value at stake, and transient risk.

01 Cold Storage / Refrigerated Warehouse
Both Devices
Harmonic DensityHigh — VFDs on every compressor and fan motor, lighting controls throughout
Billing StructurekVA demand-metered — virtually always at this scale
Equipment RiskHigh — compressor failure is catastrophic; replacement costs are substantial and downtime is revenue-critical
RecommendationTune + Stabl Power + SATIC Wire-In (3-phase) — strongest combined case in the entire facility list
Tune RoleAddresses VFD harmonics that are degrading compressor motor insulation. Direct kWh savings from I²R loss reduction on any meter.
Stabl RolePrimary surge protection at main service entry — non-degrading, lifetime rated. Eliminates transient peaks the meter reads as consumption. Series 2 or 3 standalone is sufficient for most cold storage facilities; Series 4+ for very large operations.
SATIC RoleDemand charge reduction on kVA-billed account. Supplemental secondary surge and EMI filtration downstream.
VariablesIf facility is smaller and flat-rate billed, Tune alone is the ROI driver. If budget is constrained, prioritize Tune first — compressor lifespan argument is the strongest equipment protection case in this category.
02 Supermarket / Grocery
Both Devices
Harmonic DensityHigh — VFD-driven refrigeration cases, LED lighting throughout, switching supplies in POS and display systems
Billing StructurekVA demand-metered — always at grocery scale
Equipment RiskHigh — refrigeration compressors, deli and prepared food equipment, HVAC
RecommendationTune + SATIC Wire-In — second strongest combined case; nearly identical profile to cold storage
NoteGrocery stores are among the highest harmonic-density commercial environments due to simultaneous VFD load from dozens of refrigeration cases running at all times.
VariablesSmaller independent locations may be flat-rate billed — Tune primary in that scenario. Multi-site chains: pilot one location with full M&V then portfolio roll-out.
03 Convenience Store / Fuel
Tune Primary
Harmonic DensityMedium-High — refrigeration cases, LED lighting, fuel pump electronics
Billing StructureVariable — smaller c-stores often flat-rate; larger chain accounts demand-metered
Equipment RiskMedium — refrigeration and fuel pump electronics. Outdoor exposure increases transient risk from lightning.
RecommendationTune primary. Add SATIC if account is demand-metered. Surge protection argument strengthened by outdoor exposure and pump motor cycling.
VariablesFranchise chains (7-Eleven, Casey's, Wawa) are almost always demand-metered at the corporate account level — both devices warranted. Confirm billing structure before quoting.
04 Hospitality / Hotel
Both Devices
Harmonic DensityMedium — distributed switching supplies in every room, LED lighting, laundry and kitchen equipment
Billing StructurekVA demand-metered — always at full-service hotel scale
Equipment RiskMedium-High — HVAC, laundry, kitchen equipment, elevators, AV systems
RecommendationBoth for full-service and branded hotels. Tune primary for smaller independent properties.
NoteHundreds of room-level switching supplies and phone chargers create a distributed harmonic load that accumulates significantly at the panel even when each room's individual load appears small.
VariablesRoom count, property age, and whether VFDs are present on HVAC and elevator systems. Older properties with legacy electrical infrastructure benefit more from the surge protection layer.
05 Healthcare
All Three — Lead Equipment Protection
Harmonic DensityHigh — medical imaging equipment, UPS systems, surgical LED lighting, VFDs on HVAC
Billing StructurekVA demand-metered — always at healthcare facility scale
Equipment RiskVery High — imaging equipment can exceed seven figures; control boards in life safety systems are irreplaceable on short notice
RecommendationTune + Stabl Power + SATIC — lead with equipment protection. Generator transfer switching creates severe transients. Harmonic distortion degrades sensitive imaging power supplies as a chronic liability. Stabl Series 3 with AST filtering is specifically engineered for life-support and complex digital systems.
VariablesFacility type matters within healthcare. Small clinic: Tune primary. Hospital or imaging center: both devices justified on equipment protection grounds alone, before calculating energy ROI.
06 Fitness / Gym
Tune Primary
Harmonic DensityMedium — treadmill and elliptical VFDs, LED lighting, audio and AV systems
Billing StructureVariable — larger chains demand-metered; boutique and studio fitness often flat-rate
Equipment RiskMedium — cardio equipment motor replacement is a recurring and expensive maintenance cost for facility operators
RecommendationTune primary. Treadmill motor lifespan extension is a compelling ROI argument that works on any billing structure. Add SATIC if the account is demand-metered.
VariablesLarge franchise gyms (Planet Fitness, LA Fitness) at scale are demand-metered and warrant both devices. Studio fitness with minimal cardio equipment has a weaker overall case.
07 Education (K-12 / Higher Ed)
Both Devices
Harmonic DensityHigh — computer labs, AV systems, LED retrofits, VFDs on newer HVAC, device charging carts
Billing StructurekVA demand-metered — always at institutional scale
Equipment RiskMedium-High — IT infrastructure, HVAC, and laboratory and research equipment at higher ed level
RecommendationBoth. Tune is your active DSA-submission product for this vertical. SATIC adds the demand charge reduction and surge protection layer for older building electrical infrastructure.
NoteAt higher ed, lab and research equipment elevates the equipment protection argument substantially. Older institutional buildings with aging infrastructure increase transient risk.
VariablesBuilding type within campus matters significantly. Admin and classroom buildings: both devices. Residence halls: Tune on common area panels, SATIC less compelling on individual room circuits.
08 Multifamily / Apartments
Tune — Common Area
Harmonic DensityMedium — distributed switching supplies, LED lighting, in-unit appliances, elevator VFDs on common systems
Billing StructureSplit — individual unit meters are almost always flat-rate kWh; common area service may be demand-metered
Equipment RiskMedium — elevators, common area HVAC, laundry equipment
RecommendationTune on common area panels. SATIC's demand mechanism does not apply to individual unit meters billed at flat-rate kWh. Elevator and laundry VFD harmonics make Tune effective on the master service meter.
VariablesMaster-metered properties where the owner pays all electricity change the equation significantly — full both-device case applies to the master meter. If common area service is confirmed demand-metered, add SATIC.
09 Quick-Serve Restaurant
Tune Primary
Harmonic DensityMedium-High — LED lighting, variable speed hood fans, refrigeration, POS switching supplies
Billing StructureVariable — many smaller QSR locations are flat-rate; larger footprints and chain accounts are demand-metered
Equipment RiskMedium-High — fryers, refrigeration, HVAC, hood systems. Equipment downtime is directly revenue-critical.
RecommendationTune primary — this is your core vertical. Tune stacks directly with the HVAC Optimizer: Optimizer reduces compressor cycling, Tune reduces harmonic wear on the same equipment. Strong integrated pitch.
VariablesMulti-site operators at chain account level are often demand-metered even on smaller footprint locations. Confirm billing structure and add SATIC where demand billing is confirmed.
10 Commercial / Office
Both (Mid-Large)
Harmonic DensityHigh — switching power supply density from workstations rivals industrial settings; server rooms, LED retrofits, VFDs on HVAC
Billing StructurekVA demand-metered — always at commercial office scale
Equipment RiskMedium-High — IT infrastructure, HVAC, and elevators in multi-story buildings
RecommendationBoth for mid-to-large office buildings. Tune primary for small single-tenant offices on flat-rate accounts.
VariablesLease structure matters. Owner-occupied or NNN leases where owner pays utilities: full both-device case. Gross leases where tenant pays electricity: Tune lifespan argument still applies to building systems even though the energy savings accrue to the tenant.
11 Retail (Big-Box / Strip)
Both (Big-Box)
Harmonic DensityMedium-High — LED lighting at scale is a major harmonic source at big-box fixture counts; POS systems, HVAC, refrigeration where present
Billing StructureHigh for big-box — always demand-metered. Variable for strip — anchor tenants usually demand-metered; smaller inline tenants often flat-rate.
Equipment RiskMedium — HVAC and lighting systems primary. Refrigeration where present.
RecommendationBoth for big-box. Tune primary for smaller strip tenants. LED harmonic argument is especially strong at big-box fixture counts in the thousands.
VariablesLED retrofit recency matters — older LED retrofits using lower-quality drivers often have worse THD than newer installations with better built-in power factor. Confirm this in the site assessment.
12 Mixed / Other
Assess First
ProfileToo variable to generalize. Use the assessment framework from the other 13 categories as your primary tool.
Default StartTune as the conservative default pending billing structure and load profile confirmation. Tune has the broadest applicability across billing structures and is rarely the wrong starting recommendation.
Key questionsWhat are the dominant load types? Is the account demand-metered or flat-rate kWh? What is the equipment value at stake? Map answers to the nearest comparable category in this document.
13 Warehouse / Distribution
Both — Underrated Case
Harmonic DensityMedium-High — LED high-bay lighting at scale, conveyor VFDs, dock door motors, forklift charging stations
Billing StructurekVA demand-metered — always at distribution center scale
Equipment RiskMedium-High — conveyor systems, dock equipment, refrigerated zones. Large motor switching creates significant internal transients.
RecommendationTune + Stabl + SATIC — underrated combined case. Forklift charging stations are major harmonic sources commonly overlooked. Motor-start transients from dock and conveyor equipment are real. Stabl at main service entry eliminates transient peaks that would otherwise cycle through the entire facility.
VariablesForklift fleet size drives harmonic density significantly. Cold storage zones within the warehouse elevate the compressor lifespan argument further. E-commerce fulfillment centers with high conveyor density are among the strongest combined-device cases in this category.
14 Industrial / Manufacturing
Both — SATIC Strongest Here
Harmonic DensityHigh — VFDs on virtually every motor, welding equipment, induction heating, CNC machines
Billing StructurekVA demand-metered — always. Often with explicit power factor penalty clauses written into the utility contract.
Equipment RiskHigh — CNC machines, precision motors, drives, tooling equipment. Welding and large motor switching creates severe internal transients.
RecommendationAll three — specify Stabl Series 5 or 6 + SATIC Three-Phase HD + Tune. Industrial is the strongest environment for all three devices simultaneously. Stabl primary protection at main switchgear, SATIC for demand charge reduction, Tune for harmonic current elimination. Explicit PF penalty clauses make SATIC ROI immediately calculable.
VariablesWhether the utility contract includes an explicit PF penalty clause is the single biggest variable — if yes, SATIC ROI is immediate and precisely calculable. Welding intensity and motor switching frequency drive the transient risk level and surge protection urgency.
